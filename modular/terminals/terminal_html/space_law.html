Overview

As with all secure facilities, our ISD ensures the safety of the facility and crew. Wearing sharp red uniforms* and professional smiles, security maintains order and decency aboard the station. Granted authority to maintain Space Law, they are bastions of order and professionalism.

*REV: You may have noticed some new friendly faces around the halls by now! Our Business Partners in the Transitionary Period have joined our efforts for the peaceful transition of ownership. Risk Controllers from the Proxima Centauri Risk Control Group may be seen aboard the station, and are fully vested with the same authority and respect as our Internal Security. They can be recognized by their badges and blue uniforms.

In addition to our Business Partners, there may be times where Deputies of the United Solar Conglomerate Federal Marshal Service visit the facility. While their authority is more limited aboard our facilities, deference should be given to their investigations as their prosecution of offenses may be judged outside of Internal Affairs.

SOLCOM COMPLIANCE NOTICE: As required by the Solar Combined Operations Mandate Section 102, in events of Declared Emergencies (SOS, or declared by regional authorities), command and control of the facility are ceded to SOLCOM authorities until otherwise stated. All crewmembers are REQUIRED to comply with lawful orders issued by SOLCOM Military and Law Enforcement. Failure to comply may result in charges outside of NanoTrassen jurisdiction. Immediate compliance may be forced by: Physical Compulsion, Indefinite Detainment, Summary Execution.

Procedure

In day to day operations, security personnel may encounter incidents which require detainment or citation of crewmembers. Crew are expected to comply with officers and follow lawful orders to the best of their ability. In event of arrest, the standard operating procedure is:
  • Inform subject of their Wanted status, and the reason for it. This is not required in event of visibly armed or actively ongoing criminal acts.
  • Compliant subjects are to be handcuffed and transported to the Brig without additional motivation.
  • Non-Compliant subjects are to be physically restrained and motivated into compliance.
  • Subjects are to be searched and detained for the duration applicable to their charges.
  • Subjects may be held for additional questioning related to serious offenses or incidents.

    • In the event of Brigging, crewmembers are expected to remain compliant and docile for the duration. Security personnel are responsible for the wellbeing of those arrested, and must ensure subjects' basic needs are met and no harm comes to those in captivity. Standard Brigging Procedure is:
  • Subjects are to be stripped of personal effects if to be held for significant duration.
  • Injured subjects are to be stabilized.
  • Subjects' criminal records are to be updated to reflect their infractions.
  • Subjects' criminal status are to be updated to reflect current captivity status.
  • Items removed during search or in redressing subjects into prisoner garb are to be held in evidence in the event of a criminal trial.

    • Trial and Sentencing

    Time spent in the brig is rarely desired by the average crewmember. Determining the time spent in the Brig should be based on the highest offense convicted, rather than stacking all crimes unless separated in distinct nature and time. For sentences exceeding 10 minutes or fines exceeding 1,000c, subjects may request legal representation.
    Trials should not be pursued for captivity lasting less than 30 minutes, for the sake of everyone's time as trials often take as long to arrange and undergo. Trials are encouraged in event of indefinite detention or capital proceedings, however they are NOT required.
    The Captain and Head of Security (in that order) may forgo a trial based on testimony and evidence, or confession and issue sentencing without holding a formal court trial.

    Use of Lethal Force

    At all times, security forces are expected and equipped to use every means possible to capture subjects alive and with as little force as possible.

    However, there are limits. For the safety of crewmembers and officers, it may pose an unreasonable risk to use less-lethal methods.

    Several major, uncommon situations may require the use of deadly force:
  • Significant personal risk to self or crew.
  • Heavily or exotically armed individuals may be too dangerous for less-lethal weapons, such as those in possession of explosives.
  • Non-human entities, such as drones, mutants, cyborgs, or other exotic entities may not be able to be captured by traditional means.
  • Complete collapse of order, such as large scale mutinies, boarding parties, total loss of the chain of command, or other extreme emergencies automatically authorize lethal force until order is re-established.
    •

    Anyone involved in a lethal force incident should be debriefed to ensure the reasonability and responsibility of their actions. Improper use of force may constitute a number of crimes if found to be drastic, unnecessary, or unreasonable in the moment.

    Civilian Use of Force

    While the application of force is the domain of the ISD, there may be times where crewmembers may be forced to defend themselves until rescue. However, this does not mean they may take the law and lives in their hands. The use of lethal force in self defense is highly discouraged and only authorized in extreme peril. Examples of mitigating factors include:

  • Immediate, demonstrable threat to life.
  • Non-human entities, such as drones, mutants, cyborgs, or other exotic entities which less-lethal methods are ineffective against.
  • Fatalities resulting from self defense in extreme peril, with reasonable intervention to prevent loss of life.


    • Anyone involved in a lethal force incident should be debriefed to ensure the reasonability and responsibility of their actions. Improper use of force may constitute a number of crimes if found to be drastic, unnecessary, or unreasonable in the moment.
